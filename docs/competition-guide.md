# Sequoia's pokerbot sprint rewards preparation over poker

Sequoia Capital's "No Limit: A Bot Building Tournament" is a free, in-person, student-only contest in which teams of one or two get 60 minutes to write a Python poker bot, which then plays six scored tournaments of 10,000 eight-bot sit-and-gos each. **Applications are closed as of November 10, 2026**: the Bay Area (Nov 6) and Boston (Nov 9) regionals have run, and only the Pittsburgh regional (Nov 12) and the invitation-only San Francisco Finals (Nov 15) remain, so anyone not already accepted cannot enter this edition. Sequoia organizes it with OpenAI and Citadel Securities as co-hosts, and the Finals pay $100,000, $50,000 and $25,000 per team, with $5,000 to each regional top-five team. The poker variant is secret until the build clock starts, bots must answer within one millisecond with no network access, and later tournaments count up to six times more than the first. Those constraints make runtime search and LLM play impossible and shift the edge to lookup tables, tournament-survival (ICM) logic, crash-proof code and fast measured iteration with a coding agent. The official sources are only the landing page and rules page; everything about the starter kit's internals and the revealed variant comes from unofficial participant repositories, and no regional results have been published.

## Students in the U.S. and Canada compete for roughly $250,000

Eligibility covers **currently enrolled students at any U.S. or Canadian university, plus graduates from the class of 2025 or 2026** ([Official rules](https://pokerbot.sequoiacap.com/rules.html)). The launch was U.S.-only and Sequoia expanded it to Canada shortly after ([Sequoia on X](https://x.com/sequoia/status/2115826610914103671)), which is why some early coverage still says U.S. universities only ([Digg](https://digg.com/ai/smdduv3c)). Entrants must be 18 or older, or have parental consent; teams are one or two people who need not share a school; each person may play only one regional; and employees and immediate family of the Organizer and Hosts are excluded ([Official rules](https://pokerbot.sequoiacap.com/rules.html)). Affiliation is verified by school email ([Official site](https://pokerbot.sequoiacap.com/)). No degree-level or major restriction appears in the sources. Sequoia says no poker experience is needed because "the starter kit handles the rules and the ICM math" ([Sequoia on X](https://x.com/sequoia/status/2117313159342268468)).

Entry was by a free application on the official site, with both teammates registering separately by the deadline, rolling review and limited seats ([Official rules](https://pokerbot.sequoiacap.com/rules.html); [Official site](https://pokerbot.sequoiacap.com/)). An optional "Castle Challenge", a roughly three-minute game of splitting 100 soldiers across five castles worth 1–5 points, gave top scorers priority registration ([Zeniteq](https://www.zeniteq.com/sequoia-and-openai-open-a-100k-pokerbot-tournament-6aycef); [Sequoia on X](https://x.com/sequoia/status/2115486256041963847)). Other selection criteria, seat counts and acceptance rates are unpublished, and no waitlist is documented.

**The top five teams at each regional win $5,000 per team and advance to the Finals**, and the organizer adds up to five wildcard teams chosen on scores; the Finals pay **$100,000, $50,000 and $25,000** for first through third ([Official rules](https://pokerbot.sequoiacap.com/rules.html)). That sums to $250,000 (15 regional awards plus $175,000 at the Finals), matching a partner's "over $250K" description ([Digg](https://digg.com/ai/smdduv3c)), and it implies a Finals field of up to 20 teams. Prizes are split equally between teammates, winners bear the taxes, and payment follows within 30 days of verification ([Official rules](https://pokerbot.sequoiacap.com/rules.html)). One discrepancy matters for finalists: the landing page says travel to the Finals is covered for top regional teams ([Official site](https://pokerbot.sequoiacap.com/)), while the formal rules make no travel commitment and hold participants responsible for their own expenses ([Official rules](https://pokerbot.sequoiacap.com/rules.html)). Finalists should get the travel terms in writing.

## Sixty minutes, one millisecond, and a secret variant define the contest

The format is a live sprint. Teams have 60 minutes to build a Python bot implementing a provided `Agent` interface, using a starter kit with an agent template, local simulator, ICM calculator, payout structure and blind schedule ([Official rules](https://pokerbot.sequoiacap.com/rules.html)). **The poker variant is revealed only when the build phase starts, and participants may not share it publicly before the event ends**; the Finals use a different variant ([Official rules](https://pokerbot.sequoiacap.com/rules.html)). The landing page's demos show no-limit hold'em, but they are illustrations, not a specification ([Official site](https://pokerbot.sequoiacap.com/)).

The fixed frame is public. Each scored tournament is 10,000 randomly seeded eight-bot sit-and-gos; every bot starts with 2,000 chips, blinds start at 10/20 and rise every 25 hands, and a table ends when one bot holds all chips or after 200 hands ([Official rules](https://pokerbot.sequoiacap.com/rules.html)). Points depend on finishing position, with the distribution announced at build start, and at the 200-hand cap the remaining points are split by ICM. A team's tournament score is its average points per table, and **the final score is T1×1 + T2×2 + … + T6×6** ([Official rules](https://pokerbot.sequoiacap.com/rules.html)). T4–T6 therefore carry 15 of 21 weight units, about 71% of the total, and T1 under 5%. T1 starts when the build ends, later tournaments follow 20 minutes apart, teams may submit once every five minutes throughout, and the active bot is frozen at each tournament's start ([Official rules](https://pokerbot.sequoiacap.com/rules.html)). That allows at most about four submissions between tournaments.

Runtime limits are severe. Bots run in isolated sandboxes without network access and cannot call OpenAI tools or any external service for gameplay; **each decision must return within one millisecond**; the maximum file size is 100 MB; and an error or timeout becomes an automatic fold, or a check where available ([Official rules](https://pokerbot.sequoiacap.com/rules.html)). Bot state resets at every table and opponents are anonymized, so learning about opponents is confined to a single table of at most 200 hands. The rules specify no memory limit, Python version or permitted library list.

On AI tools, the rules state that "Teams may use Astra and Codex to write, debug, and refine their bots during the build phase", permit public documentation, make OpenAI tools optional, forbid AI from making live gameplay decisions, and hold teams responsible for reviewing and testing generated code ([Official rules](https://pokerbot.sequoiacap.com/rules.html)). "Astra" is not identified in any source read. Whether non-OpenAI assistants or pre-written code are formally allowed is not stated; one entrant team's repository lists Claude as a team member and contains a pre-built prep folder, which shows what entrants did, not what the rules bless ([nnicholas-c/poker_sequoia](https://github.com/nnicholas-c/poker_sequoia)). Disqualification covers collusion, copying another team's code, platform interference and submission-endpoint misuse; disputes go to a Gamemaster before the next tournament starts; and participants keep ownership of their code ([Official rules](https://pokerbot.sequoiacap.com/rules.html)).

### What entrants report about the kit and the Bay Area game

The following details are unofficial. Sequoia publishes no public repository or API documentation, and one of the two main sources is an explicit local mock built partly on guesses. Entrants describe an SDK whose core call is `act(self, state, memory) -> (Action, bytes)`, with a 64 KB per-table memory blob as the only persistent state because each team runs as a pool of copies; a `poker-agent` command line tool with `check`, `play` and `pack`; example bots `check_or_fold` and `min_raiser`; and a ban on importing `poker_engine` or `pokerkit` ([nnicholas-c/poker_sequoia](https://github.com/nnicholas-c/poker_sequoia); [aniketxdey/pokerbots](https://github.com/aniketxdey/pokerbots)). They also report that a raise outside the legal range is not clamped and counts as a failed decision, and that a 100 ms per-table time bank sits behind the 1 ms limit. A Bay Area team recorded that regional's variant as **hold'em with three hole cards, in which each live player discards one card and exposes one after the flop**, with default table points of 50/30/20 for the top three, making 12.5 points par ([nnicholas-c/poker_sequoia](https://github.com/nnicholas-c/poker_sequoia)). A second repository describes the same game second-hand but says the timing of the discard was unconfirmed ([aniketxdey/pokerbots](https://github.com/aniketxdey/pokerbots)). That mock also lists blinds rising every 20 hands, a 250-hand cap and "at least 5,000" iterations per round, all of which conflict with the official rules; the official figures should govern. The Boston variant was not found anywhere.

## Lookup tables and survival math beat poker AI research here

The research frontier does not fit this format. Systems such as Libratus and Pluribus pair a precomputed equilibrium blueprint with real-time search ([Brown & Sandholm, Science 2019](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.aay2400)), and the leading commercial solver likewise "solves it in real-time" ([PokerNews](https://www.pokernews.com/news/2026/04/gto-wizard-ai-outperforms-gpt-5-and-grok-4-in-new-benchmark-51020.htm)). One millisecond of Python allows a handful of dictionary lookups and comparisons. Participants found that even live Monte Carlo equity fails: such bots "often use their full 1 ms and get cut off, so they mostly check or fold" ([aniketxdey/pokerbots](https://github.com/aniketxdey/pokerbots)), and another entrant dropped live Monte Carlo because it "gave no reliable gain and risked the time limit" ([EthanXu07/poker-bot-overview](https://github.com/EthanXu07/poker-bot-overview)). LLMs cannot play at all under the sandbox rules, and their poker instincts are weak anyway: in a March 2026 benchmark the best frontier model lost about 16 big blinds per 100 hands to a solver, with models producing unbalanced, call-heavy ranges ([GTO Wizard Benchmark](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.23660)). That benchmark is run by the solver's vendor and measures heads-up play, so it transfers only loosely.

The workable design is a precomputed-table bot: canonicalize the hand, look up equity, compare fold, call and raise by expected value, and choose thresholds by stack depth in big blinds, players remaining and position. One entrant built exactly this as an interpretable three-tree bot and found equity-based hand ranking beat the Chen formula in his tests, 43.5 versus 40.9 points per table ([EthanXu07/poker-bot-overview](https://github.com/EthanXu07/poker-bot-overview)). Because scoring is by finishing place, not chips, tournament logic matters more than chip-EV theory; standard push/fold charts need ICM adjustment near payout jumps ([ICMIZER](https://icmizer.com/en/blog/nash-calculator-and-nash-equilibrium-strategy-in-poker)). The same entrant's sharpest lesson cuts the other way, though: with a top-heavy points table "finishing first matters more than finishing high", and a version that folded about 77% of hands preflop "often finished second but rarely first" until it gained endgame aggression ([EthanXu07/poker-bot-overview](https://github.com/EthanXu07/poker-bot-overview)). All performance figures from participants are self-reported simulations, not regional outcomes.

Robustness comes before cleverness. Every exception, timeout or illegal raise turns into a check or fold, so the highest-value code in the bot is a wrapper that catches errors and clamps raises to the legal range. Variant brittleness is the other common failure: "Hold'em-only bots often error or fold in other variants" ([aniketxdey/pokerbots](https://github.com/aniketxdey/pokerbots)). Since the variant is hidden, the asset worth preparing is a generator that rebuilds equity tables for a new rule set in minutes, not the tables themselves. One entrant shipped a single standard-library file of about 165 KB and exceeded 1 ms in 1 of 283,227 decisions under heavy load ([EthanXu07/poker-bot-overview](https://github.com/EthanXu07/poker-bot-overview)); standard library only is the safe assumption, because third-party package availability in the sandbox is unconfirmed.

The scoring weights dictate the game plan. Ship a legal, crash-proof baseline early in the hour, because the first tournament is worth little and a broken late submission wastes it. Then treat each 20-minute gap as one hypothesis, one measured test and one submission, and avoid replacing a working bot with an untested one before T5 and T6. Measurement needs discipline because poker is noisy: one participant's mock puts standard error at roughly 20/√tables points and finds 100–500 tables enough to rank bots ([aniketxdey/pokerbots](https://github.com/aniketxdey/pokerbots)), which implies about 1,600 tables to resolve a half-point difference. Another entrant promoted a change only if it beat the incumbent by more than 1.5 standard errors and won again on a fresh batch, tuned 49 thresholds with an evolution strategy against the exact competition score, and trained against varied opponents including naive ones ([EthanXu07/poker-bot-overview](https://github.com/EthanXu07/poker-bot-overview)). Evidence on LLM-written bots supports steering the coding agent with an explicit architecture: in the Husky Hold'em benchmark, weaker models produced more defensive bots ([NeurIPS 2025](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2025/122372)), which is the over-folding failure described above. A two-person team works best split between a strategy lead who reads the variant and decides what to test, and an engineering lead who owns the harness, timing and submissions.

## Conclusion

For anyone not already accepted, this edition is a spectator event, and the useful move is to email pokerbot@sequoiacap.com about future editions and watch for results after November 15; no second edition has been announced. For accepted Pittsburgh entrants and finalists, three questions deserve an answer from the organizers before the clock starts, because the public rules are silent on each: whether pre-written code and tables may be brought in, whether assistants other than Codex and "Astra" are permitted, and which third-party Python packages the sandbox accepts. The answers determine whether preparation means tooling or only rehearsed prompts and knowledge.

The deeper point is that the contest measures something other than poker skill or poker-AI expertise. A secret variant, a one-hour build and escalating weights reward teams that can direct a coding agent, test honestly under noise and keep improving for 100 minutes after the first deadline. A participant should also read the two public entrant repositories with care: they are the only window into the kit and the Bay Area game, but they contradict the official rules on several parameters, and the Finals will use a variant nobody has seen.
