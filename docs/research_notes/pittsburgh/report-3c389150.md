# Tournament 3c389150: heads-up improvement, partly offset before heads-up

**The heads-up behavior changed substantially, and its observed results improved.**
Points rose from **12.782 to 13.120 per table**, but weaker results at 5–8 players
offset much of the heads-up gain. No bot code was changed for this analysis.

The requested first-10,000-table download actually contains **10,003 complete,
unique scored hero tables**. All were analyzed; this is a prefix of the tournament,
not its full results. Baseline is the complete 10,000-table `242e19e4` download.

## Results

| Metric | Previous 242e19e4 | New 3c389150 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Points/table | 12.782 | **13.120** |
| First-place rate | 7.78% | **11.73%** |
| Second-place rate | 17.92% | 12.41% |
| Third-place rate | 17.58% | 17.67% |
| Paid/top-three rate | 43.28% | 41.81% |
| Reach a hand starting heads-up | 25.31% | 23.67% |
| Win after reaching heads-up | 30.15% | **48.56%** |
| Mean chip share on entering heads-up | 37.51% | 42.06% |
| Automatic fallbacks | 0 | **0 / 1,337,227 decisions** |

This is a shift toward **more firsts and fewer seconds**, with fewer paid finishes
overall. First-place frequency increased about 51% relative to the baseline.
The descriptive rank among teams observed at our tables improved from 60/112 to
54/112. Neither figure is an official leaderboard rank.

The overall gain is **+0.338 points/table**, with a rough independent-table 95%
interval of **-0.128 to +0.804**. Overall superiority is not yet established by
this unpaired sample. Opponents, runouts, and potentially bot versions differ.

## What changed heads-up

| Behavior | Previous | New |
|---|---:|---:|
| BB folds to non-all-in raise, 10–25 effective BB | 71.3% | **20.4%** |
| BB re-raises/shoves versus that raise | 9.2% | **47.4%** |
| BB calls a shove, <=10 effective BB | 15.9% | **49.1%** |
| Button raises/shoves, 10–25 effective BB | 63.3% | **91.5%** |
| Button raises, >25 effective BB | 71.2% | **96.2%** |

These are large observed policy-frequency changes, not just a different win total.
For example, the new 10–25 BB defense sample includes 3,961 opportunities, and the
<=10 BB shove-call sample includes 4,903 opportunities. The download does not
identify the exact uploaded source, so it cannot establish which code edit caused them.

Heads-up big-blind equity changed from **-0.625 to +0.044 points per original
table**. Small-blind equity was nearly unchanged, +0.252 to +0.264. Almost the
entire heads-up gain is therefore located in big-blind hands.

The hands with no voluntary preflop contribution cost **-0.291 points/table** in
heads-up versus -1.091 previously: an improvement of 0.80. Preflop shove-hand
results decreased from +0.582 to +0.474. This is consistent with less surrender
of blinds being the main improvement, rather than better shove runouts. Categories
measure whole-hand realized equity changes; they are not causal action values.

We did enter heads-up with more chips, but conversion improved both when starting
ahead (63.8% to 76.5%) and behind (15.4% to 33.2%). Those broad groups are still not
matched for exact stack size or opponent strength.

In all five full 2,000-table blocks, heads-up equity was positive: **+0.324,
+0.366, +0.255, +0.187, +0.410** points per original table. Every corresponding
baseline block was negative. The three extra returned tables are excluded only
from this block comparison, not from the headline totals.

## What offset the gain

| Players alive | Previous equity change/table | New | Difference |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 5–8 | +0.951 | +0.478 | **-0.473** |
| 3–4 | -0.297 | -0.166 | +0.130 |
| 2 | -0.373 | +0.308 | **+0.681** |
| Total above starting 12.5 | +0.282 | +0.620 | +0.338 |

The most useful next check is **deep-stack preflop responses at 5–8 players**.
With a starting stack >20 BB, our first response facing a raise changed as follows:

| Action | Previous | New |
|---|---:|---:|
| Fold | 89.3% | 89.8% |
| Call | 3.8% | **7.5%** |
| Re-raise, including shove | 6.9% | **2.7%** |

These counts cover 301,651 and 290,836 opportunities respectively; depth here is
our stack, not effective stack. Continuation frequency barely changed, but calls
replaced re-raises. Early-table VPIP was essentially flat (17.23% to 17.30%).
That makes a blanket increase in opening frequency an unfocused response.

Within the early-table outcome categories, preflop-only hands fell from +0.544
to +0.318 points/table, postflop call-only hands worsened from -1.035 to -1.153,
and no-voluntary-preflop-investment hands worsened from -3.566 to -3.728.
Those categories do not isolate deep-stack calls, so they do not prove the
call/re-raise shift caused the full early-table loss.

**Next experiment:** preserve the new heads-up behavior and test deep-stack
call-versus-re-raise decisions with matched hands/opponents. Avoid rolling back
the heads-up change just because the total-score gain is modest. No strategy
change is promoted or uploaded by this report.

- Zero automatic fallbacks; zero unfinished tables. All public/private pair,
  card-alignment, chip-conservation, and equity-reconciliation checks passed.
- 86,371 recorded rivers seen and 18,303 recorded scoops in this prefix.
- Audit elapsed time: 21.06 seconds while the detailed analyzer ran concurrently.
- These are replay outcomes, not all-in-adjusted equity. Optional organizer
